Persistence (Mudelsee 2002 least-squares estimator)
---------------------------------------------------
tau, native uneven series                    11.5 yr
tau, 5-yr bins                               19.5 yr
tau, 5-yr bins minus LOWESS background       8.6 yr
Excursions persist for roughly a decade. Binning raises tau because the 5-yr
average itself adds correlation, so the native value is the better physical
estimate. The declustering window for the threshold models is set to 10 yr,
close to tau, so that peaks closer than one persistence time count as a single
event. The residual series still carries multi-decadal memory, visible as
slowly decaying autocorrelation, so effective sample sizes are much smaller
than the number of bins.

Binary segmentation uses a BIC penalty and a minimum segment of 150 yr.
Autocorrelation makes such tests liberal, so breaks are read as regime
descriptions, not as formal detections. The segment means show that the mean
state shifts by more than 1 permil over the record, which is the main reason a
single stationary extreme value model needs the checks in step 8.


Spectral content (Lomb-Scargle vs AR(1) red noise)
--------------------------------------------------
Red-noise levels come from 1000 AR(1) surrogates generated on the native
sampling times with the estimated tau. Local maxima above the 95 % level:

  period   409.2 yr                          power 0.116  (also above 99 %)
  period   783.8 yr                          power 0.072
Isolated exceedances of the 95 % level are expected by chance across many
frequencies, so only peaks above the 99 % level deserve attention. A peak near
409 yr clears the 99 % level, but the record contains only about six such
cycles, so it is read as suggestive multi-centennial variability, not as a
periodic driver. Most variance sits in red-noise-like power at long periods,
consistent with regime behavior.
